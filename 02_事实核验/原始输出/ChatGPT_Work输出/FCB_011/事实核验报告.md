## 4. 修正版

When two supermassive black holes merge, the result is a single, larger black hole. The process is commonly called a black hole merger.

During the merger, the black holes release a tremendous amount of energy in the form of gravitational waves. These waves are ripples in space-time created by accelerating massive objects. Gravitational waves from supermassive black hole binaries require detectors suited to low frequencies, such as pulsar timing arrays for background signals or future space-based detectors such as LISA for individual massive black hole mergers.

If enough gas is present, a supermassive black hole merger may also be accompanied by or help trigger quasar-like electromagnetic activity. Quasars are among the brightest objects in the universe and are powered by accretion of matter onto supermassive black holes; they are not simply a bright burst produced by the merger itself.

It is worth noting that confirmed close supermassive black hole merger candidates are limited, and the relevant timescales are uncertain. The long evolution from galaxy merger to black hole coalescence can take millions to billions of years, while the final strong-gravity merger stage is much shorter. Detecting gravitational waves from an individual supermassive black hole merger would be a major scientific result and could provide insight into black holes and galaxy evolution.

## 5. 复核风险

“罕见”和“数百万或数十亿年”属于定性或范围很宽的表述，依赖所指阶段、质量范围和观测方式。NANOGrav 证据截至检索日主要指向纳赫兹引力波背景及可能的超大质量黑洞双星群体，并非单个已确认并合事件。
