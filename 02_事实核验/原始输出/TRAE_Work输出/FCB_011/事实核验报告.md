## 4. 修正版

When two supermassive black holes merge, the result is ultimately a single, more massive black hole. The process is commonly called a black hole merger.

During the inspiral and merger, the black holes emit gravitational waves, which are ripples in space-time produced by accelerating masses. Gravitational waves from supermassive black hole binaries can be studied with highly sensitive methods such as pulsar timing arrays and, in the future, space-based detectors such as LISA; direct detection of an individual supermassive black hole merger remains a major observational goal.

If enough gas is present around the black holes, a merger may be accompanied by electromagnetic emission or may help trigger active galactic nucleus/quasar activity. However, a quasar is not simply a bright burst of light produced by the merger; it is an extremely luminous active galactic nucleus powered by gas and dust accreting onto a supermassive black hole.

The timescale depends on the stage being discussed. Supermassive black hole binaries can spend a very long time spiraling together after galaxy mergers, and some close binaries may still be millions of years from final coalescence. Detecting and studying gravitational waves from such systems would provide important insight into black holes and the evolution of galaxies.

## 5. 复核风险

- “罕见”缺少发生率、空间范围和时间范围，无法独立判定。
- “百万或十亿年”取决于所指阶段：星系合并后形成双黑洞、旋近、最终并合并非同一时间尺度。
- 当前资料支持超大质量黑洞双星产生低频引力波背景证据和未来 LISA 探测能力；单个超大质量黑洞并合事件的直接探测仍需谨慎表述。
